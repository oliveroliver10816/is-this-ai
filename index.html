Deep research · verdict

“Is This AI?”

The hook is good and the instinct is right. But the poll is the one thing every platform has written a rule against — and the platform prints the answer on your own video before the viewer can guess.

2026-08-05 · verdict: BUILD IT — with the question changed from a poll to a reveal
4 of 8 research agents died on a session limit; the unverified gaps are listed at the bottom, unfilled.
The call

Keep the name. Kill the poll. Ship the reveal.

“Is this AI?” as a question you leave hanging is a format Meta lists by name as unmonetizable, on three separate grounds, in the exact programme you already earn from. It also depends on an ambiguity the platform destroys with a badge and the EU now requires you to destroy yourself.

“Is this AI?” as a question you answer in the same clip — hook for three seconds, then show the tell: the hands, the physics, the reflection, the melting text, the audio seam — is the same hook, keeps every argument in the comments, and is the one shape that clears Meta's, TikTok's and YouTube's originality rules simultaneously. It also walks straight into a $2M TikTok fund that is paying for precisely this content.

One hard constraint before anything ships: MiniMax H3 output cannot legally carry a global brand. Design on H3, render the winners on Wan 2.2.

FINDING 01

The platform answers your questionstructural

The premise is that the viewer can't tell. Every major platform now tells them, automatically, using metadata you don't control.

“Our new label… will appear to viewers as a label below your caption in the video when they watch it.” — and: “Can I edit or remove the label after posting your content? No, you cannot edit or remove the label after posting your video at this time.”
tiktok.com/creator-academy/article/ai-generated-content-label · published 2026-04-30

TikTok reads C2PA Content Credentials and, in its own words, “invisible watermarks… that only we can read”. So the label is not a choice you make — it is a detection you receive. And the alternative is worse:

“TikTok may take down AI-generated content that's not labeled.” Unlabelled realistic AIGC is ineligible for the For You feed — and “content ineligible for the For You feed will not be eligible for the Creator Rewards Program.”
TikTok Community Guidelines (AIGC) + creator-academy/article/creator-rewards-program
The one genuinely good piece of news in this section

Carrying the label costs you nothing on TikTok, and they say so twice: “Applying the label will not have an impact on the engagement with your content, provided that your content isn't violative and meets our For You feed eligibility standards” and “Content will not be demoted or restricted solely because the AI-generated content setting is enabled.”

So the label is not a punishment. It is simply an answer key stapled to your quiz. Which is fatal to a guessing game and completely harmless to a teaching one.

FINDING 02

Meta names your exact formatblocks the money

This is the finding that decides it. Your proven earner is Facebook Content Monetization. Its policy has a “prohibited formats” list, and the format you described is on it — close to word for word.

Static image polls — “Content posted for the sole purpose of increasing engagement by asking people to react to questions posed by the content.”

Text montages — “Content that primarily displays still or moving images with overlaid text.”

Engagement bait — “Content that incentivizes people to click a link or respond to a post through likes, comments or shares.”
facebook.com/business/help/1348682518563619 — Content Monetization Policies, prohibited formats + prohibited behaviors

And separately, the overlay itself is named as not enough to make content original:

Minor editorialization — insufficient: “Adding only an on-screen text caption or title” · “Simply describing (in text, on-screen, or voiceover) what happens in a video with no other meaningful additions” · “Basic compilations that involve splicing third-party clips together without adding your own narrative or new information”.
facebook.com/business/help/262834734651607 — Original content guidelines on Facebook

Four independent axes, each of which blocks monetisation on its own. Meta's own framing line is “content suitable for Facebook in general is not necessarily appropriate for monetization.”

Meta writes down the way through, too

Qualifying content includes “Remixes or overlays that feature your own on-screen presence as the focus of the video, and also add new information, commentary, or storyline improvements beyond simply narrating what happens, making facial expressions, or watching along.” That sentence is the entire recommendation in Finding 06.

⚠ Separately: FCM is still invite-only with no published rate. A brand-new page cannot enrol; the only published route in is Creator Fast Track at 100k+ followers on IG/TikTok/YouTube. So Facebook is a destination for this brand, never its starting line.

FINDING 04

The law arrived three days agotiming

EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations started applying on 2 August 2026. You are proposing to build a brand on ambiguity in the same week ambiguity became a disclosure offence.

“Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake, shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated.” Providers “shall ensure that the outputs… are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated.”
EU AI Act, Article 50 — obligations applicable from 2026-08-02

The deployer is the one posting. Note the shape of the carve-out, because it is the most useful sentence in the whole Act for you:

For “artistic, creative, satirical, fictional” work the obligation is limited to “disclosure of the existence of such generated or manipulated content in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment”.
EU AI Act, Article 50(4)

Read that carefully: a reveal at the end of the clip is a disclosure that doesn't hamper enjoyment. A permanently unanswered “is this AI?” is not. The law is pointing at the same format change the monetisation policies are.

FINDING 05

The platform ranking invertsuseful

The intuitive order for a short-form AI brand is TikTok → Reels → Shorts. On the rules as written, it is the reverse.

YouTube's live risk section is “Generic or Repetitive Content” — it catches templated mass-production, not AI as such. Nothing on any of these three platforms bans AI content. All three ban unoriginal content. AI is never the trigger; sameness is.

TikTok's 5-strike tripwire is the real operational risk

“If your account has 5 or more ineligible videos in the past 30 days, it may be disqualified”, and 5 total account violations makes you permanently ineligible for the rewards programme. For a format that produces several borderline clips a week, that is not a tail risk — it's a schedule.

FINDING 06 · THE RECOMMENDATION

Turn the poll into a revealdo this

One change to the format. It keeps 100% of the hook, keeps the argument in the comments, and clears every gate above at once.

✕ What you described

Clip runs. Overlay: “Is this AI? 👇 comment your guess”. No answer, ever.

Meta: prohibited format + engagement bait + minor editorialization.
TikTok: label spoils it; “low quality / unoriginal” exposure.
YouTube: templated, no added value.
EU: undisclosed deepfake.

✓ Same hook, different second half

Clip runs. Overlay: “Is this AI?” — hold 3 seconds. Then you answer it and show the tell: the sixth finger, the physics that doesn't settle, the reflection that doesn't match, the text that melts, the audio seam.

The comments now argue “I caught it in 2 seconds” vs “no chance” — a stronger fight than a coin flip, because people defend their own eye.

What that second half is, in each rulebook's own words:

Meta → “add new information, commentary, or storyline improvements” = the qualifying route, verbatim
YouTube → “explains how the creator added to the content” = the reused-content escape, verbatim
TikTok → originality is a term in the payout formula; the analysis is your own input, not spliced clips
EU AI Act → “disclosure… that does not hamper the display or enjoyment” = a reveal, exactly
The platform's AI badge → stops being a spoiler and becomes the answer key, arriving after your reveal
And there is a named buyer for this framing

TikTok is funding it: a $2M AI literacy fund for creators making For You feed content that “teaches people about AI literacy and safety” (TikTok Newsroom, 19 Nov 2025). That is the first hard commercial signal in this entire research pass, and it points at “Is this AI? — here's how to tell”, not at “Is this AI? — you'll never know”.

FINDING 07

Costs you should price inrisks

  • Audiences are getting a dial to see less of you. TikTok is testing an AI-content control in “Manage topics” so viewers can choose how much AIGC they see. A brand that self-declares as AI opts into being filtered. The literacy framing partly escapes this — it is content about AI, not only content made of AI.
  • “Questionable from the beginning” has a shelf life. The moment your audience knows the account is AI, the question stops being a question. The reveal format is the version that survives that transition, because being taught a tell doesn't get old the way a coin flip does.
  • The name space is taken. isthisai.com, isthisaiornot.com, realorai.com, isitai.com and aiornot.com are all registered (RDAP, checked today). On X, @isthisai and @isitai resolve; @isthisai_, @isthisaiornot and @realorai return 404. ⚠ TikTok, Instagram and YouTube handle checks were inconclusive from this box — they return 200/302 for every string, taken or not. Those need a real browser before you commit to a name.
  • “AI or Not” is already a live AI-detection product. Naming collision risk — unverified, the agent covering it died.
  • One page, one hook, forever. A single-format account has no second act. Worth deciding up front whether “Is This AI?” is the brand or the first series inside a broader AI-literacy brand that can outlive the gimmick.
HONESTY

Everything above is a rules analysis; it does not tell you whether the audience still cares. This is worth re-running first.
  • Facebook CM payout rates for this content type, and whether Meta reduces distribution of AI-labelled content the way it says it doesn't on TikTok.
